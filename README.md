## A little about the creature behind the code

I make things for places that don't quite exist.

Virtual worlds, strange little tools, shaders, interactive stories, and occasionally the infrastructure holding an entire virtual rave together. I'm a developer, environmental artist, and storyteller with a particular weakness for ambitious ideas and technical limitations that really ought to know better than to challenge me.

Most of my projects begin with *"Wouldn't it be cool if..."* and end several weeks later with me deep in Unity, wondering how a five-minute idea became a small engineering department.

I like abandoned places, analog horror, impossible architecture, lights that behave just right, and worlds that feel like they were here before you arrived. But I also love the other side of VR: the music, the crowds, the people who turn a collection of polygons into somewhere that matters.

Somewhere between those two things is where you'll usually find me.

## Signals from the archive

### V//H//S — The Corrupted Tape
*Playable prototype · continuing development*

My little love letter to the things hiding in the static. An analog-horror experience for VRChat, built around an unsettling camera system, reactive environments, spatial audio, and the feeling that something is watching from just outside the frame.

The first prototype reached **almost 25,000 visits in a single weekend**. Since then I've kept exploring what the experience can become across PCVR and mobile, without letting the technical machinery get in the way of the atmosphere.

### Alder Creek Incident
*Story and worldbuilding · in development*

An Alaskan town. An incident in 1986. A handful of tapes that probably should have stayed buried.

Alder Creek is the narrative world taking shape around my horror work: an evolving archive of characters, false histories, strange recordings, and choices whose consequences aren't always immediately apparent. Much of it still lives in writing and design documents rather than a finished experience.

### OmniSurface AI
*Working systems prototype · in development*

Apparently giving a creature the ability to walk on the floor wasn't quite enough.

OmniSurface is my experimental VRChat AI system for entities that can navigate **floors, walls, and ceilings**, hunt a target, lose sight of them, search, and find other ways around an environment. Its core chase, search, and relocation behaviours have been tested; it's still very much an evolving monster.

## Strange things on the workbench

Not everything I make is a complete world. Sometimes the interesting part is the ridiculous system hiding behind one small detail.

- **Shaders, fog, and lighting: ongoing experiments.** Custom HLSL, stage fog, depth effects, stylized avatar lighting, and ways of making VRChat environments react to light without asking a standalone headset to perform miracles. I spend an unreasonable amount of time thinking about how shadows *feel*.
- **VirtuaLabs Forest System: experimental tooling.** Procedural vegetation and large-scene rendering experiments, playing with LODs, culling, atlases, and how to make a dense environment feel alive without making the frame rate disappear.
- **Candle Shrine: working cross-instance prototype.** An experiment in letting people light candles in separate VRChat instances and see a shared state. A tiny virtual ritual, held together by a much less tiny synchronization problem.
- **Shared World Experience Framework: in development.** An attempt to stop rebuilding the same foundations for every world: entry sequences, audio routing, subtitles, localization, configuration, and the little bits of polish that make an experience feel intentional.

A lot of these experiments never become standalone releases. They're still part of the journey, and they tend to find their way into something else eventually.

## Somewhere after midnight

I've spent a good part of my life in virtual nightlife, where stages are made of shaders, entire venues can exist for a single weekend, and thousands of people somehow manage to make a digital space feel very real.

Some of the things I'm proudest of weren't made for a repository. They were made for a crowd, a moment, or a performance that would only happen once.

### SANCTUM & VirtuaMotion: ghosts in the machinery
*Production-deployed technology · past collaboration*

One of my longer-running obsessions is **VirtuaMotion**, an in-house motion-capture and performance system I've been building in one form or another since *Gathering: Beginnings* in 2024. The goal was to give performers more freedom to exist *inside* a virtual stage, rather than merely appear on a screen.

In **April 2026, VirtuaMotion reached its first full production deployment at [SANCTUM](https://sanctumvr.com/)**. Seeing something I'd tinkered with for years become part of a real performance was a little surreal. Its continuing development explores both live and pre-produced performances, entirely within VRChat.

I also worked on SANCTUM's scripting and technical implementation, including the period around its fifth-anniversary productions. It was a meeting of music, movement, theatrical spaces, and some particularly stubborn engineering problems—and a reminder of why I love building for virtual performance.

### Sly Fest 2026: another world, another weekend
*Festival development · completed event*

I contributed development, implementation, and production-tooling support to **[Sly Fest 2026](https://slyfest.com/)**, the VR music festival that took place in October. Gathering put its own 2026 festival on pause to help support Sly Fest's production.

It's one of the things I love about this scene: different teams and communities coming together to build something bigger than any one of them could have made alone.

### Gathering: worlds that only exist when we're together
*Past events · ongoing creative history*

From **Gathering: Beginnings (2024)** to **Gathering: Adventure (2025)**, I've helped build the worlds and systems behind large-scale, multi-instance virtual events. That work has taken me through environment art, backend infrastructure, technical production, and the orchestration of a great many moving parts.

One especially memorable night was Gathering's **2025 New Year's Eve event in VRChat's official NYE world**. Our instances filled up, and the queue stayed with us through much of the night. Strange as it sounds, seeing people waiting to enter a place we'd helped build is its own kind of magic.

Along the way I've also been involved with **Paradise Lost, Ministry Promotions, RIFT_VR, VirtuaFest, GearFest**, and other corners of the virtual event scene. Sometimes I'm working on stage systems; sometimes it's infrastructure or production planning. The part I remember isn't a job title. It's the moment the lights come on and the world fills with people.

## Breadcrumbs I've left on GitHub

Some of the more shareable pieces of the chaos:

- **[Vee's Toolbox](https://github.com/vesturo/Vees-toolbox)** - My collection of oddly specific VRChat scripts and fixes. The repository description is *"me documenting my insanity's c:"*, which is probably the most accurate documentation I've ever written.
- **[VRC Image Deployer](https://github.com/vesturo/vrc-image-deployer)** - My adaptation of VRChat's community image-loading example for hosting world images and posters through GitHub Pages.
- **[VLAM VPM Package Converter](https://github.com/VirtuaLabs-online/VLAM_VPMPackageConverter)** - A VirtuaLabs utility for turning Unity asset folders into VPM-compatible packages.
- **GroupSync / GSLink** - An earlier community tooling project exploring Discord-to-VRChat account linking, onboarding, and synchronization. Part of my older experiments rather than an active showcase.

Most days the tools behind everything are some combination of **Unity, UdonSharp, C#, HLSL, Blender, JavaScript, Node.js, Python**, and whatever server-side glue is necessary to make the idea work.

## One last thought

> Sometimes people should stop asking themselves if they could, and instead start asking themselves if they should.

I say that a lot for someone who keeps trying to teach virtual monsters to climb ceilings.

Not everything here is finished, public, or sensible. Some things are experiments, some become tools, and some become stories. I think that's half the fun of creating things in virtual spaces: there's always another strange little possibility waiting in the dark.
